# DeepSentinel Progress Snapshot - 2026-06-24

## One-Scheme Thesis

DeepSentinel is now framed as a single defense: select ordinary, high-density
retrieval images whose CLIP geometry makes them retrievable by private triggers.
The defense does not rely on visible random keys, OCR-visible text, or a bundle
of auxiliary mechanisms. This keeps the AAAI story focused on the filtering
failure mode of ImageSentinel-style keyed sentinel images.

## Current Evidence

The evidence now forms a three-step chain.

1. Synthetic sweep: visible-key sentinels are removed immediately, hidden
   outlier sentinels are removed by mild density filtering, and DeepSentinel
   survives all tested density drops across 10 seeds.
2. COCO CLIP retrieval: on both COCO-1k and COCO-5k, DeepSentinel survival stays
   at 1.0 through 50% density pruning. On COCO-5k, the hidden-outlier baseline
   collapses to 0.0 survival from 35% pruning onward.
3. Aggregate RAIG proxy: with eight private triggers, threshold two, false match
   rate 0.001, and generation evidence retention `g=0.5`, DeepSentinel proxy TPR
   remains 0.9375-0.9648 across pruning levels, with proxy FPR 2.79e-05.

The latest AAAI draft now includes compact COCO-5k and aggregate RAIG proxy
tables, plus a regenerated PDF.

## Key Limitation

The current selection-only method demonstrates filter resistance more strongly
than semantic precision. The selected COCO sentinels look ordinary, but some
private trigger matches are broad rather than visually exact. This is acceptable
for the present paper if we state the contribution as a collateral-damage
filtering result, not as a full semantic-control solution.
